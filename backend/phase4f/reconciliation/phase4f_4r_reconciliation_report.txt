2. CRITICAL FINDING: BROKEN TOKEN SPAN ALIGNMENT
======================================================================

SEVERITY: CRITICAL — INVALIDATES ALL TRAINING RESULTS

In run_experiment.py, the QADataset.__getitem__ method tokenizes
inputs WITHOUT passing return_offsets_mapping=True:

  encoding = self.tokenizer(
      ex["question"], ex["context"],
      max_length=self.max_length, truncation=True,
      padding="max_length", return_tensors="pt"
  )

This causes encoding.get("offset_mapping") to return None, triggering
the fallback heuristic:

  start_token = min(ans_start_char, len(ctx_ids) - 1)
  end_token = min(ans_end_char - 1, len(ctx_ids) - 1)

This uses CHARACTER offsets as TOKEN indices — fundamentally wrong.

IMPACT MEASUREMENT:
  With broken heuristic:    6/876 (0.7%) training labels are correct
  With correct alignment: 249/876 (28.4%) would decode correctly
  Truncated (even correct): 1/876 (0.1%)

The model was trained to predict WRONG token positions for 99.3%
of all training examples.

6. A1 DISTILBERT ASSESSMENT
======================================================================

Status: INVALID — trained with broken labels

Reported: EM 0.7%, F1 6.9%

The A1 model was trained from distilbert-base-cased (NOT the
production model) with randomly initialized QA head and BROKEN
span labels. The training loss decreased (5.36 -> 3.62) because
the model learned to minimize cross-entropy on the wrong labels,
not because it learned to extract correct answers.

The 0.7% EM represents accidental matches — the model occasionally
predicts a span that happens to match the answer text by chance,
despite being trained on wrong positions.

Root cause: BROKEN_SPAN_ALIGNMENT (missing return_offsets_mapping)

======================================================================
7. A2 BERT-BASE ASSESSMENT
======================================================================

Status: INVALID — trained with broken labels

Reported: EM 0.0%, F1 9.3%

Identical root cause as A1. BERT-base has 110M parameters (vs
66M for DistilBERT), but both were trained with the same broken
span alignment. The slightly higher F1 (9.3% vs 6.9%) reflects
BERT's larger capacity for partial token overlap, not meaningful

Root cause: BROKEN_SPAN_ALIGNMENT

======================================================================
8. A3 DEBERTA-V3 ASSESSMENT
======================================================================

Status: INVALID — NaN training instability

Reported: EM 0.0%, F1 0.0%

Training collapsed from epoch 1 with NaN loss, even with fp16
disabled. The model never learned and defaulted to always
abstaining (outputting [CLS] position).

Possible causes:
  - DeBERTa's relative position attention is sensitive to
    learning rate and may require different tuning
  - The broken span labels may have caused extreme gradients
  - 918 examples may be insufficient for DeBERTa's capacity
  - The NaN loss could also be caused by the combination of
    broken labels + DeBERTa's attention mechanism

The checkpoint was saved from NaN-weight training and is
non-functional.

12. SCIENTIFIC CONCLUSION

OUTCOME: PROTOCOL_INVALID_REQUIRES_RERUN

The Phase 4F.4 experiment has a protocol violation (broken span
alignment) that invalidates the A1/A2/A3 training results. The
A0 baseline is valid but not directly comparable to A1/A2/A3
because they use different base models.

The reported "catastrophic forgetting" interpretation is WRONG.
The models did not forget pretrained knowledge — they were trained
with corrupted labels from the start. The training was not
"fundamentally flawed" in concept but was IMPLEMENTATION-BROKEN.

KEY EVIDENCE:
1. 99.3% of training labels were wrong (char offsets as token indices)
2. Production model is distilbert-base-uncased, A1 used distilbert-base-cased
3. No train/test split (evaluation on training data)
4. Checkpoint selection saves final epoch only (no best-model)
